Pressurized Tank with Suspended Load: FEM Analysis in ANSYS

SolidWorks · ANSYS Mechanical · Static structural analysis

Project Description

This project presents a finite element analysis of a steel pressurized tank subjected to an internal pressure of 1 MPa and to the weight of a 200 kg pump-motor assembly mounted on the top nozzle.

The objective is to evaluate the structural response of the tank, particularly the total deformation and von Mises stress, and to investigate the behavior of the numerical solution in the region where higher stress levels are expected.

Study at a Glance

  • Software: SolidWorks, ANSYS Mechanical
  • Analysis type: Static structural
  • Internal pressure: 1 MPa
  • Suspended load: 200 kg point mass on the top nozzle
  • Material: Structural steel

Part 2 — Geometry Preparation in ANSYS

The SolidWorks geometry was imported into ANSYS and divided into two regions.

This separation was introduced because higher stress levels were expected in a particular region of the structure. Dividing the geometry allows this area to be treated more locally if additional mesh refinement is required.

Part 3 — Material, Boundary Conditions and Loading

The model was set up in ANSYS Mechanical for a static structural analysis using structural steel as the material.

Boundary Conditions and Loads

The tank was constrained at its supports. The different loads were introduced using load steps to progressively apply the actions acting on the structure.

  • Gravity: accounts for the weight of the tank itself.
  • Internal pressure: a pressure of 1 MPa is applied to the internal surfaces of the tank. A cross-section view was used to facilitate the selection of these surfaces.
  • Pump-motor assembly: represented by a 200 kg point mass located at the top nozzle. This accounts for the suspended equipment without explicitly modelling the complete pump-motor assembly.

Part 4 — Results and Adaptive Mesh Refinement

After solving the model, the main structural results were examined through the total deformation and von Mises stress distributions.

Initial Results

Initial solution before mesh refinement
Quantity Value
Maximum von Mises stress 61.1 MPa
Maximum total deformation 0.05 mm
Tensile yield strength 250 MPa

The initial solution gives a maximum von Mises stress of 61.1 MPa. This value is below the tensile yield strength of the steel, which is 250 MPa.

The corresponding maximum total deformation is approximately 0.05 mm.

Adaptive Mesh Refinement

An adaptive mesh refinement study is performed to investigate the influence of mesh refinement on the numerical results, particularly in the region where the highest stress levels are observed.

The refined results will be compared with the initial solution to evaluate the stability of the calculated stress and deformation and to determine whether further refinement significantly changes the results.

Conclusion

The initial finite element analysis provides an assessment of the structural behavior of the pressurized tank under internal pressure, gravity and the additional load associated with the 200 kg pump-motor assembly.

The initial solution gives a maximum von Mises stress of 61.1 MPa and a maximum total deformation of 0.05 mm. The maximum stress remains below the 250 MPa tensile yield strength of the material.

The adaptive mesh refinement study will be used to further assess the stability of these numerical results.
