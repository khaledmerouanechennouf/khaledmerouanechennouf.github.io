Pressurized Tank with Suspended Load: FEM Analysis in ANSYS

Project Description

This project focuses on the finite element analysis of a tank subjected to an internal pressure of 1 MPa and to the weight of a pump-motor unit (200 kg) mounted on its top nozzle, through which the fluid enters. The objective is to determine the stress and deformation fields, locate the critical zones, and verify that the structure remains safe with respect to the yield strength of the material. The work is organized into three parts, each illustrated with a dedicated video.

Technical Tools:

SolidWorks - 3D geometry design
ANSYS Mechanical - Static structural analysis
Finite element method
Mesh convergence and analytical validation

Part 1 — Geometry Design in SolidWorks

The tank was designed in SolidWorks, including the cylindrical shell, the end caps, the support base and the top nozzle through which the fluid enters. The geometry was built with the simulation in mind, keeping the features that influence the stress distribution (nozzle junction, supports) and avoiding unnecessary details that would complicate the mesh.

Part 2 — Import into ANSYS and Material Definition

The SolidWorks model was imported into ANSYS Mechanical for a static structural analysis. The default Structural Steel material was assigned to the tank: Young's modulus of 200 GPa, Poisson's ratio of 0.3, density of 7850 kg/m³ and yield strength of 250 MPa. The linear elastic behavior of this material is sufficient here, since the objective is to verify that stresses remain below the yield limit.

Part 3 — Meshing, Boundary Conditions, Solution and Validation

The model was meshed with a local refinement around the nozzle junction, where stress concentrations are expected. The tank is held by a fixed support at its base. An internal pressure of 1 MPa is applied on all the internal faces, and the weight of the pump-motor unit (about 1962 N for 200 kg) is applied on the nozzle flange, together with the standard earth gravity acting on the structure. After solving, the von Mises stress and the total deformation were analyzed, and the safety factor was computed with respect to the yield strength. The results were validated by comparing the stress in the cylindrical shell, far from the nozzle, with the analytical thin-wall formula σ = pR/t, and by checking the stability of the results with successive mesh refinements.
